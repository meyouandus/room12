Why now

The market for live fan experience is moving

Clubs, venues and rights holders seek new approaches to spectatorship, new tools for audience insight, and new content and experiences, in the venue and beyond it. A live concert or match can now be streamed into immersive venues anywhere. LCR creatives across music, games, film and immersive can meet this demand.

US$100m

Sony Pictures’ stake in Cosm’s immersive venues in 2026: investors are backing new ways to watch live events.

100,000sf

Apple Corps’ Beatles experience, opening in Liverpool in 2028: a local buyer for immersive content and tools.

£79,000

GVA per job in the City Region’s creative cluster, 25% above the LCR average, with 15,000+ jobs and 29% growth in five years.

Sources: LCR Growth Plan 2025–2035; press reports, 2026

The gap

What holds the region’s creative firms back

Commercialisation

Workforce

Too few trained immersive producers and technicians.

What ROOM12 is

Two parts, one route to market

ROOM12 raises the productivity and scale of the region’s creative firms through AI and immersive technology, with live music, sport and games events as the place they prove and sell what they make.

Part one

Live Event Spectator Lab

R&D into how people watch, feel and take part in live music, sport and games. Firms get in through access vouchers and grants, build skills on live projects, and prototype and test in LEXIS with a direct link into the arena.

Part two

Venture development and commercialisation

Led by Form, with a free IP clinic: turning what works in the Spectator Lab into products, licences and first sales, through to routes to market.

The Spectator Lab runs from LEXIS, an immersive studio in Room 12 at the M&S Bank Arena.

Spectator Lab · How it works

A partner challenge, from problem to proven product

  1. Set a challenge

    A club, venue, brand or rights holder names a real problem worth solving.

  2. Creative SMEs

    Win grants and studio time to answer it.

  3. Prototype

    Built in LEXIS with industry-grade kit and technical support.

  4. Test live

    Tested in real live-event settings with LEX and Adlib.

  5. First access

    The partner who set the challenge gets first look at what works.

Form leads venture development and commercialisation: pricing, licensing and first sales. A free IP clinic helps rights holders and firms protect existing IP and create new IP together.

Intellectual property

A free IP clinic for both sides of every challenge

Partners bring valuable IP; firms bring their own; some will create new IP together. ROOM12 extends the MusicFutures IP Clinic, an established, free model at LEX Campus, to every challenge.

Rights holders and IP owners

  • Challenge briefs set out what content, data and brand can be used, and how
  • Terms agreed before work starts

Creative firms

  • Free guidance on copyright, trade marks, designs, contracts and licensing
  • Firms keep the IP they create

Co-creation

  • Who owns what in shared IP, agreed up front
  • How shared IP is licensed and income shared

Education first, disputes last.

Standard rights, data and IP terms are agreed once, so small firms do not negotiate alone and partners know where they stand. About the MusicFutures IP Clinic

Support for firms

One route from first idea to first sale

  1. Join

    Open calls in all six local authorities, with access vouchers for studio and kit time.

  2. Adopt

    Staged AI adoption grants (c.£2,500) and training on live projects.

  3. Build

    Equipment grants (c.£10–25k) for kit firms keep, and studio time in LEXIS with technical support.

  4. Test

    Challenge fund grants and commissions, tested in real live-event settings.

  5. Sell

    Led by Form. Venture development, pricing, licensing and first sales.

Demand-led: firms can enter at any stage. Grants at a 50% intervention rate, matched by firms in cash, staff time or in kind. Sizes indicative.
